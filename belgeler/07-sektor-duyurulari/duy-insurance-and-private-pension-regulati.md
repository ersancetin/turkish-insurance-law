Chairman’s Foreword
By the end of 2021, the total assets of the insurance,
reinsurance, and private pension sectors had expanded by
39.3% up to 429 billion TL, from 307.8 billion TL at the end
of 2020.
Since its inception on June 5, 2020, our
Authority has been operating with the
goals of facilitating the growth of the
insurance and private pension sector,
which plays a significant role in our
economy; enhancing its contribution to our
economy;
protecting
the
insured/participants; fostering a healthy
and sustainable competitive environment;
and ensuring that it is one of the sectors
with high international integration. Despite
challenging conditions and uncertainty
brought by the pandemic in 2020, we were
able to establish numerous significant
regulations in sectoral areas concurrently
with the establishment of the Authority.

Our
Authority
achieved
numerous
milestones in the insurance and private
pension sectors in 2021, including the
integration of children under the age of 18
into the individual pension system (IPS),
giving early outcomes as a result of the
parents’ desire to give their children a
secure future. We have taken many
steps to transform the IPS into a
“savings
pool”
for
people,
going
beyond its role of safeguarding the
level of income in the pension system.
The most important steps taken to
accelerate this transformation mainly
include
increased
state
support,
participants’ capacity to meet cash
demands for specific needs without
logging out of the system, and acquiring
loan under the right circumstances by
putting the IPS funds up as collateral.

The most important regulations we
have made in the scope of insurance
mainly include the clarification of
certain long-standing issues around
motor liability insurance (compulsory
traffic insurance). The amendments to
the General Terms and Conditions of
Traffic
Insurance
provided
predictability about claims for both the
insured and beneficiaries as well as the
insurance
companies.
Thus,
by
creating the Special Risks Management
Center and broadening the coverage
of State-Supported Credit Insurance
under the auspices of that Center, we
managed to build a significant pillar of
our initiatives to improve the access of
SME’s to funding.

We
took
a
significant
step
in
digitalization
by
modifying
the
legislations as needed in order to align
the insurance and pension sectors with
the
higher
level
of
digitalization
brought on by the pandemic. Our
efforts to improve sector’s compliance
with
international
standards,
especially those relating to climate
change, will continue apace.
Through
our
effective
oversight,
enforcement, and supervision activities
that we carried out also in 2021, we
have
assessed
the
new
license
applications, the complaints passed on
to our Authority, and the compliance of
individuals and organizations operating
in our sector with the insurance and
private pension legislation, we took any
necessary action.
By the end of 2021, 65 companies in
total,
including
6
life
insurance
companies, 15 pension companies, 3
reinsurance companies, and 41

non-life insurance
companies,
were
active in the Turkish insurance and
private pension market. Compared to
the figures for 2020, the amount of
premiums generated in the sector
increased by 27.6%, reaching 105.3
billion TL. Life insurance accounted for
16.8% of the industry’s total premium
production, while non-life insurance
accounted for 83.1%. Motor liability
insurance (compulsory traffic insurance)
accounted for the highest portion of
non-life
insurance
overall
(26.6%),
followed by land vehicles (16.3%), fire
and
natural
disasters
(16%),
and
sickness/health insurance (14.9%).

By comparison to 307.8 billion TL at the
end of 2020, the active total of the
insurance,
reinsurance,
and
private
pension industry climbed by 39.3% to
429 billion TL by the end of 2021. The
sector’s share within the financial sector
was realized at a 4.2 percent level. These
results are significantly influenced by
pension
funds,
and
they
also
demonstrate the value of actively using
the insurance and private pension sector
as saving incentives. By the end of 2021,
the members in the individual pension
system (IPS) had funds totaling up to
205.5 billion TL, of which 45.4 billion TL
were non-interest funds. Besides, a state
subsidy amounting to 22.3 billion TL was
contributed to the IPS by the end of
2021.

I. INFORMATION ABOUT THE AUTHORITY
The Board is the decision-making body
of the Authority and consists of five
members appointed by the President of
the Republic, including the Chairman,
Deputy Chairman, and three board
members,
all
appointed
by
the
President of the Republic.
1. Legal Status of the Authority
Insurance and Private Pension Regulation
and Supervision Authority (the “Authority”)
was established to carry out the tasks
regarding the regulation and supervision of
the insurance and private pension sectors
pursuant to the Presidential Decree No. 47
and dated 18.10.2019 on the Organization
and Duties of the Insurance and Private
Pension Regulation and Supervision Authority.
The Authority has a public legal personality,
and administrative and financial autonomy.
The Authority is headquartered in Istanbul,
and is affiliated with the Ministry of
Treasury and Finance.
2. Primary Duties and Powers of the Authority
Pursuant to Article 4 of the Decree No. 47,
the primary duties and powers of the
Authority are as follows:
a) To carry out the tasks and to exercise the
powers related to insurance and private
pension stipulated in the Highways Traffic Law
dated 13.10.1983 No. 2918, Private Pension
Savings and Investment System Law dated
28.3.2001 No. 4632, Agricultural Insurance
Law dated 14.6.2005 No. 5363, Insurance
Law dated 03.06.2007 No. 5684, Turkish
Commercial Code dated 13.01.2011 No.
6102, the Catastrophe Insurance Law dated
09.05.2012 No. 6305, and in other relevant
legislations.
b) To prepare, implement, monitor and
direct the execution of the legislation
concerning insurance and private pension,

Our Authority strives to assure
complete
compliance
with
international
standards
in
the
supervisions on the insurance and
private pension sector, to create a
risk-based supervision system, and
to make the most of informatics in
order to conduct our supervisions
more effectively, more quickly, and
more efficiently.
In its supervisions, our Authority
continues
to
make
efforts
to
increase the level of conformity
with the Insurance Core Principles
(ICP) updated by the International
Association of Insurance Supervisors
–
IAIS)
to
ensure
complete
compliance with the international
norms and criteria, mainly including
the ICP published by the IAIS, and
prioritizes the said principles in its
Supervision Guidelines created by
Supervision Offices.

For a detailed description of supervision
and inspection processes as well as for
standardization of supervision products
emerging from said activities, the
processes regarding the creation of
supervision schedules, execution of
supervisions, issuance, review and
completion of supervision products
have been specified with sub-
regulations, which are also reviewed,
and updated at specific intervals. On the
other hand, to help the insurance
specialists and standardize the
inspections, a separate guideline has
been established for each and every
area of supervision that falls under the
purview of planned supervision.
Guidelines for supervision are regularly
reviewed and revised to guarantee
compliance with both applicable laws
and international standards.

.
In order to make our supervisions
transparent and to make their outcomes
predictable and traceable for supervised
individuals and institutions, our Authority
continues to make efforts to post on our
official website the guidelines and report
formats provided.

has been constituted to inspect and
review the reports in the presence of
the Supervision Departments. In this
context, the reports produced by the
specialists
are
reviewed
by
the
Committee
and
any
errors
and
deficiencies are notified to the specialists
so that they might be duly addressed.
The

have
the
opportunity to establish a significant
control and quality standard through a
thorough internal review process of
reports, a detailed control of any
available errors and deficiencies, and a
control of the compatibility of their
contents with the legislation and other
internal instructions.
